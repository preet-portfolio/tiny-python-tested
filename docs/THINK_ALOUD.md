# Think-aloud sessions: script

Five people, 10 minutes each: three kids aged 8–12 and two adults who don't write code. Run
them on the current site first, then on the new first screen, with the same script, so the two
can be compared.

You need: a phone or laptop with the site open on the home page, a timer, this sheet. Record
the screen and voice only if the person (and for a child, their parent) agrees. Don't record
faces or names; write "Kid A, 9" and so on.

## Before you start (say this)

> "I'm testing a website, not you. You can't get anything wrong. Please say out loud whatever
> you're thinking: what you're looking at, what you expect to happen, what's confusing. If you
> go quiet I'll ask 'what are you thinking?'. I can't help you until the end."

Then stay quiet. Only ever say: "What are you thinking?" or "What do you expect will happen?"

## Tasks

Start the timer when the page appears. Don't read the tasks out word for word if the child
reads slowly; say them.

| # | Task | Stop at | Note down |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Have a look around. What do you think this website is for?" | 30 s | Their words. Did they mention a toy, a game, a computer program, rules? |
| 2 | "Make one of the toys do something." | first output, or 60 s | Seconds to first toy answer. Where they hesitated. Anything they tried that didn't work. |
| 3 | "Try to make it do something silly or wrong." | 90 s | Did they find a toy getting something wrong? Did they laugh? |
| 4 | "Can you find out what a gremlin is?" | 60 s | Did they reach the gremlins? Did they wait for them? Where they gave up. |

## Teach-back (after the tasks)

Ask, and write their answer word for word:

1. "Tell me what a gremlin did."
2. "Who caught it, and who didn't?"
3. "If you made a toy, how would you know it really works?"

**Pass** when the person, without help, says something like "it changed one thing and the
book's rules didn't notice, but the real-life rule did". Exact words don't matter.

## After all five

Count, per screen element, how many people stalled on it. An element two or more people
stalled on goes on the cut list as "move" or "remove", unless it's the aha moment itself.
